On the Ground in Indianapolis

What we actually see in Indianapolis homes.

Indianapolis is a city of old housing stock, and old housing stock is where plumbing gets interesting. Inside I-465 you’ll find Craftsman bungalows in Irvington and Meridian-Kessler, double-brick homes in Fountain Square, and converted lofts downtown — most of them pushing or past a century old.

In pre-1960 homes we routinely find original galvanized-steel supply lines, corroded nearly shut from the inside, and cast-iron drains on the back half of their life. Clay sewer laterals running out to the street are the other constant — mature trees and root intrusion go hand in hand around here, and a backed-up main is one of our most common Indy calls.

Marion County is served by Citizens Energy Group, and the older core still runs a combined storm-and-sanitary sewer — which is why a hard rain can push water back into low basements until the DigIndy tunnels finish their work. Add hard Central Indiana water that scales out water heaters early, and there’s always something to stay ahead of.

Flood readiness

Flood prevention and sump-pump help in Indianapolis.

In older Indianapolis neighborhoods, heavy rain can stress both a lower-level sump system and an aging sewer lateral. We can test or replace a sump pump, review its discharge routing, and help sort out whether water in a basement points to drainage, a backup, or both.

For homes affected by combined-sewer conditions, a flood-prevention visit can include backup-system discussion and a backwater-valve evaluation. It is a practical way to prepare before the next storm instead of guessing after water shows up.
